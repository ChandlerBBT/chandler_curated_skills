# Valuation Discipline

## Method Selector

Choose valuation methods from business economics, not habit.

For every method, state:

- Why applicable.
- What would make it invalid.
- What cross-check will be used.
- Which inputs are facts, estimates, assumptions, or missing.

Common methods:

- DCF: use when cash flow path and capital structure can be estimated. Avoid when free cash flow has no credible path, terminal value dominates, or the business model is unstable.
- PE or earnings multiple: use when earnings are normalized and comparable. Avoid near cycle peaks, accounting distortions, or one-off earnings.
- PB or NAV: use when asset value or regulated capital drives value. Avoid when book value does not reflect economic value.
- PS or EV/revenue: use for early-stage or margin-transition situations only with a credible margin bridge.
- EV/EBITDA or EV/EBIT: use when operating profit is comparable after lease, minority, associate, and capital-intensity adjustments.
- PEG: use only when growth quality and duration are credible.
- SOTP: use when business segments have different economics and valuation anchors.
- rNPV: use for milestone-based assets with estimable probability, timing, cost, and payoff.
- DDM: use for stable capital-return businesses.
- Asset revaluation: use when replacement value or liquidation value is more relevant than current earnings.
- Order or backlog methods: use only when order quality, conversion, margin, cancellation risk, and timing are verifiable.

## Scenario Builder

Never start from a desired target price. Build scenarios from drivers.

Order:

1. Revenue: volume, price, mix, customers, capacity, utilization, take rate, or other unit drivers.
2. Gross margin: scale, pricing, input costs, product mix, competitive pressure.
3. Expense ratio: sales, R&D, G&A, fixed versus variable cost.
4. Tax rate: statutory, incentives, geography, deferred items.
5. Capital expenditure: maintenance, expansion, depreciation link.
6. Working capital: receivable days, inventory days, payable days, contract balances.
7. Cost of capital: currency, rates, beta, leverage, risk premium.
8. Terminal value: long-term growth or exit multiple; test plausibility.
9. Exit multiple: cycle, quality, growth, and market regime adjustment.

Build base, bull, and bear cases with explicit assumptions. Do not create bull/bear by only moving discount rates.

## Signal Integration

Map financial-quality lights into valuation:

- Green: standard method, normal margin of safety, normal position cap.
- Yellow: raise margin of safety, increase discount or haircut, reduce position cap, require closer review.
- Red: use pressure case, widen downside, cut position cap or pause new buying.
- Double red: stop valuation, mark `[UNRELIABLE]` or `[MISSING]`, and do not publish a target price.

## Misuse Guards

Check:

- Terminal value share is not dominating the model.
- Bull case does not require impossible market share, margin, or reinvestment.
- Bear case reflects real operating stress, not token downside.
- Peer multiples are adjusted for growth, margin, accounting, geography, leverage, and capital intensity.
- One-off gains/losses do not drive normalized value.
- Sensitivity table changes the drivers that matter most.
- Upside/downside is a range, not false precision.

## Output

Include:

- Primary valuation method and cross-check method.
- Base/bull/bear value range.
- Implied assumptions.
- Sensitivity table or verbal sensitivity.
- Upside/downside and payoff symmetry.
- Margin of safety.
- Key missing inputs.
- Valuation kill conditions.

If the key inputs are missing, write what data is needed rather than forcing a target.
