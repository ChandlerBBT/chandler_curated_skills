# Technical, Event, And Risk Playbook

## Technical Role

Technical analysis is a timing and risk-control layer. It does not replace financial quality, valuation, or catalyst verification.

Use it to answer:

- Is the security in an investable stage?
- Is relative strength confirming the thesis?
- Is the entry point close enough to invalidation?
- Where is the stop?
- Is the pattern failing?

## Technical Frameworks

Combine the following as useful:

- Stage analysis: accumulation, uptrend, distribution, downtrend.
- SEPA-style leadership: strong earnings/sales, price strength, institutional-quality demand, and clean risk point.
- VCP-style contraction: volatility and volume contraction before breakout.
- CANSLIM-style checklist: current growth, annual durability, new catalyst, supply/demand, leadership, sponsorship, market direction.
- Relative strength: compare against benchmark and relevant peer group.
- Volume and price: breakout volume, failed breakout, dry-up, gap behavior, support violation.
- Moving averages: medium and long-term trend, failure levels, retest quality.

## Entry Logic

A valid entry window normally requires:

- Market regime allows the risk.
- Fundamental thesis is intact.
- Financial-quality screen has no unresolved red flag.
- Price structure is constructive.
- Relative strength confirms.
- Risk point is close and explicit.
- Catalyst or evidence window is defined.

Trial position is appropriate when evidence is promising but incomplete. Full position requires multiple independent confirmations.

## False Breakout Red Flags

Treat these as risk reducers:

- Breakout volume is weak.
- Relative strength fails to confirm.
- Contraction structure is loose or incomplete.
- Benchmark or peer group does not confirm.
- Breakout occurs around a major event with speculative behavior.
- Short-term leverage or speculative participation rises too quickly.
- Price returns below the breakout or support level quickly.

## Catalyst And Policy Validation

For every catalyst, classify:

- Type: earnings, product, order, regulation, capital return, restructuring, cycle turn, management change, or macro variable.
- Surprise: new information or already expected.
- Transmission: how it affects revenue, margin, cash flow, cost of capital, or multiple.
- Timing: when it should show up in data.
- Verification: which metric proves it.
- Expiry: what makes the catalyst stale.

Sell-the-news red flags:

- Price rose materially before the announcement.
- Information was widely discussed before announcement.
- Positioning and expectations became one-sided.
- Announcement confirms expectations but adds little incremental economics.
- Price gaps up and reverses quickly.
- Beneficiary set is broad and not scarce.

## Kill Criteria

Use a traffic-light system:

| Level | Typical trigger | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| Green | Normal volatility | Follow plan |
| Yellow | Small drawdown, repeated weak closes, fading volume, relative weakness | No add, update stop, quick review |
| Orange | Larger drawdown, partial thesis break, failed breakout, guidance downgrade | Reduce materially, complete detailed review, no averaging down |
| Red | Hard stop, full thesis break, unreliable financials, major governance/regulatory event | Exit, cooling-off period, rule writeback |

Hard rule: averaging down is forbidden after orange or red triggers unless a fresh thesis is written and the original kill trigger is explicitly resolved.

## Position Sizing

Size from:

- Confidence.
- Downside to stop.
- Liquidity.
- Volatility.
- Correlation with existing exposures.
- Market regime.
- Financial-quality light.
- Event gap risk.

Trial positions should be small enough that being wrong teaches without damaging the portfolio.
