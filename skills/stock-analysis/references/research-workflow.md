# Research Workflow

## Mandate Intake

Capture:

- Target or screening universe.
- Market and currency.
- Time horizon.
- Intended action: idea generation, buy/hold/sell, position sizing, hedge, watchlist, or review.
- Benchmark and risk limits.
- Required depth and data constraints.

If the user has not supplied enough context, proceed with clearly labeled assumptions rather than stalling, unless the missing item changes the decision materially.

## Market Regime Controller

Score the regime before evaluating single names.

Dimensions:

- Liquidity: rates, credit, market turnover, funding stress.
- Earnings revision: breadth and direction of estimate changes.
- Risk appetite: relative performance of high-volatility and long-duration assets.
- Trend structure: index trend, breadth, failed breakouts, follow-through.
- Policy direction: supportive, neutral, restrictive, or uncertain.

Regimes:

- Offense: allow higher beta and stronger growth exposure with defined stops.
- Balance: prefer quality, evidence, valuation discipline, and measured sizing.
- Defense: favor cash flow certainty, lower volatility, and smaller trial positions.
- Risk-off: protect capital; reduce new risk unless evidence is exceptional.

Conflict rules:

- Strong policy plus weak price structure allows only trial sizing.
- Strong chart plus weak financial quality is not investable without repair evidence.
- Cheap valuation plus worsening fundamentals is a value trap until an inflection is verified.
- Crowding alone is not a sell signal; crowding plus earnings deterioration is.

## Candidate Pool

Use a multi-factor funnel:

1. Exclude structurally uninvestable names: liquidity too low, governance flags, unreliable reporting, severe dilution, unresolved regulatory risk.
2. Rank quality: ROIC/ROE, margin durability, balance-sheet strength, cash conversion, governance.
3. Rank growth: revenue, earnings, cash flow, reinvestment runway, revisions.
4. Rank valuation: absolute, relative, historical, and implied expectations.
5. Rank momentum: trend, relative strength, breadth, volume quality.
6. Rank catalyst: earnings, product, order, regulation, capital return, restructuring, cycle turn.
7. Rank risk: volatility, drawdown, correlation, liquidity, event gap.

Keep a watchlist tier for candidates that are strong but missing one proof point.

## Fundamental Research

Answer:

- What creates value?
- Who pays, why, and how durable is the willingness to pay?
- What constrains growth?
- What expands or compresses margins?
- What is the capital intensity?
- What would make the business worse even if revenue grows?
- What is consensus likely missing?

Separate:

- Company quality.
- Stock attractiveness.
- Timing.
- Position size.

## Earnings Review

After results:

1. Identify reported facts and reporting period.
2. Compare against expectations.
3. Separate headline beats from quality beats.
4. Map changes to model lines: revenue, margin, expense, working capital, capex, tax, dilution, net debt.
5. Decide whether thesis is strengthened, weakened, unchanged, or unresolved.
6. Define next datapoints and action.

## Thesis Tracking

Maintain:

| Date | Thesis point | Evidence | Status | Next test | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Statuses: proved, partially proved, unchanged, weakened, disproved, missing.

## Decision Categories

Use explicit action language:

- Buy/add: evidence, valuation, timing, and risk all support action.
- Trial position: thesis promising but proof incomplete.
- Hold: thesis intact, entry not attractive or position already sized.
- Trim: upside/risk deteriorated or position too large.
- Exit: kill criteria triggered or better evidence disproves thesis.
- Watch: quality or setup interesting but missing proof.
- Pass: insufficient edge, unfavorable risk/reward, or unfixable red flag.
